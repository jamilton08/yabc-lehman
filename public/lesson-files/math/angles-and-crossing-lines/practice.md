# Extra practice: angle rules

Same calculator as the lesson, but nothing here is recorded. Hints are free, and you can take as many tries as you need. Before you pick a rule on each one, say it out loud: *equal*, *180*, *90*, or *360*.

## Warm up

```calc
title: One step

1. angles linear: 72 | x
2. angles vertical: x | 118
3. angles complementary: 41 | x
4. angles around: 150 | 95 | x
```

## Set it up and solve

```calc
title: Linear pairs and vertical angles

1. angles linear: 2x + 20 | 3x + 10
2. angles vertical: 5x - 8 | 3x + 22
3. angles linear: 7x | 2x + 18 | find 2
4. angles vertical: 8x + 4 | 10x - 20
5. angles complementary: 3x | 2x + 15
```

## A little harder

```calc
title: More than two angles

1. angles around: 2x | 3x | 4x | 90
2. angles around: x + 30 | 2x | 3x | find 2
3. angles linear: x/2 + 30 | x
   > x/2 is ½x. Combine ½x and x to get 3x/2, subtract 30, then multiply both sides by 2/3: x = 100, so the first angle is 80°.
```

## Mixed review

```calc
title: Mixed review

1. angles vertical: 9x - 25 | 6x + 20
   > 3x = 45, so x = 15 and each angle is 110°.
2. angles linear: x + 15 | 2x | 3x - 15 | find 2
   > Three angles on a line: 6x = 180, x = 30, and the angle marked 2x is 60°.
3. angles complementary: x/2 + 20 | x - 5 | find 2
   > 3x/2 + 15 = 90, so 3x/2 = 75 and x = 50. The second angle is 45°.
4. angles around: 3x | 2x + 40 | 3x | 2x + 40 | find 2
   > 10x + 80 = 360, so x = 28. Two crossing lines make two vertical pairs: 84°, 96°, 84°, 96°.
```

## Sneak peek: triangles

The three angles inside a triangle always add up to 180°. That is next lesson's rule, but you can already solve these.

```calc
title: Triangles (preview)

1. angles triangle: 2x | 3x | 4x
2. angles triangle: x + 10 | 2x + 5 | 45 | find 2
3. angles triangle: x | x | 2x + 20 | find 3
   > 4x + 20 = 180, so x = 40. The two base angles are 40° each and the top angle is 100°.
```
